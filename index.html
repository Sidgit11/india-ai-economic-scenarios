India · The economy in 2030

What will India's economic future look like?

Will AI make India rich faster? Will it take away the office jobs a generation studied for? Both? We took the Anthropic Institute's model of how AI changes an economy and ran it on India.

Same model, same assumptions about AI. Only the economy is different. How we built this is at the end.

How the model thinks

An economy is made of tasks

Every job is a bundle of tasks. AI leaves some tasks alone, helps with some, does some on its own, and creates a few new ones. Take a loan officer at a bank branch in Indore.

UnchangedAI helpsAI does itNew task

Now count every loan appraisal, every KYC check and every field visit across every branch in the country. Add every other job. That is India's economy: about ₹346 lakh crore of work a year. What AI does to the tasks decides what it does to the economy.

Why India is not the US

Out of 100 Indian workers, 23 do office work. In the US, 62 do.

The model only lets AI touch office and professional work: managers, professionals, technicians, clerks and sales staff. Everyone else is left alone. That one fact drives most of what follows.

Three possible futures

The same three scenarios, run on India

The scenarios differ in how good AI gets, how widely it is used, and how much of the work it does on its own. The settings are the Anthropic team's. The results are India's.

Modest

A normal technology

AI touches a third of office work and is used on a fifth of it. Think Copilot in IT firms and banks. The effect is real but hard to see in the national numbers.

Finding 1 · Growth

AI grows India's economy in every scenario, but by a third of the US gain

Only 23 of every 100 workers are exposed, so the upside is smaller. In rupees, the extreme case adds an economy the size of Maharashtra by 2030.

Finding 2 · Jobs

Fewer workers have to move than in the US. The ones who do fall harder.

AI shrinks office work and grows demand for everything else. Workers have to cross over, and crossing over is slow. In India the crossing is from a salaried desk to farm, site or shop work, at a fraction of the pay.

Finding 3 · Pay

Average pay rises a little. Office pay falls, and in the extreme case it falls for real.

Pay in construction, transport, farming and care work rises because a richer economy buys more of what they do. Office pay goes the other way. In the extreme case it ends 14% below today's level after inflation.

Finding 4 · Who gets the money

The pie grows. A bigger slice goes to capital.

Today 53 paise of every rupee India produces goes to workers and 47 to capital. When AI does a task, the pay for that task moves from a worker to whoever owns the AI.

Beyond the model

Six things about India the model cannot see

The model is deliberately simple. For India, some of what it leaves out is the story.

India exports office work

IT and back-office firms earn $246 billion a year from clients abroad. Whether that work gets automated is decided in a US bank's budget meeting, not in India. Indian adoption can lag and the jobs still go.

The AI capital is mostly foreign

When pay moves from workers to capital, the model books it as Indian capital income. Much of it is a bill to Microsoft, Google, OpenAI or Anthropic. GDP rises by these numbers. National income rises by less.

Job losses arrive as jobs never offered

India's workforce grows 1.5% a year. A shrinking office sector gets there by not hiring. It shows up as graduate unemployment, already 11 to 13%, before it shows up as layoffs.

The other 77% is not a soft landing

The model moves a displaced worker into "other work" at that group's pay. In the US that group includes electricians and nurses. In India it is mostly farm, site and daily-wage work at a third to half of office pay.

A safety net for one group only

MGNREGA guarantees rural manual work. Nothing covers an unemployed software tester in Pune. Holding office incomes at their no-AI level in the extreme case would cost about 6% of GDP, half of what the Centre collects in tax.

Indians still spend mostly on goods

Food is 40 to 47% of household spending. An AI dividend gets spent on physical things and in-person services. That is why the model raises pay for construction, transport and farm workers. An AI boom shows up on building sites first.

What to watch

The future is not decided

Every setting in the model is something we can measure. The next two years of Indian data will say which scenario we are in. The paths only separate after 2027.

Office-worker unemploymentQuarterly PLFS bulletin, by education. From 6.6% toward 9% by early 2028 is the substantial signature.
IT and BPM net hiringNASSCOM's annual figure was +133,000 in FY25. Two negative years in a row is the entry-gate channel showing up.
How much AI does on its ownIndia already leans toward delegating whole tasks. More of that is the setting that separates substantial from extreme.
Adoption beyond large firms47% of large enterprises have AI in production. Small firms and the informal economy are near zero. That gap is India's diffusion rate.
How we built this

This page is an adaptation of Scenarios for our Economic Future by the Anthropic Institute (Korinek, Jones, Sacher, Cotter and McCrory, working paper 2026-02, September 2026). Their model treats the economy as a set of tasks that either people or AI can do, with two groups of workers (office and professional work, which AI can touch, and everything else, which it cannot), a capital market, an ideas channel, and a monthly job-search block with slow-moving office wages.

We rebuilt the model from the paper's equations and checked it by reproducing the paper's US results (their Table 3) to within 0.1 percentage points on every line. Then we changed only the inputs that describe the economy, listed below, and left every assumption about AI as the paper set it. The 2030 amount of work AI affects is scaled so that the same fraction of office work is affected as in the US: 32%, 48% and 80% of it. The explorer above runs the same model on all 4,375 combinations of answers, with the paper's survey settings for the inputs the questions do not cover.

Limits to keep in mind. The model pays everyone in a group the same wage, so it cannot show the pay cut a displaced office worker takes. It is a closed economy with Indian-owned capital, so it misses exported office work and foreign-owned AI. It has no informal sector and no robots, so the 77% it treats as safe will not stay safe past 2030. And, as the paper says, it has no business cycles, no policy response and no catastrophic risks. These are not forecasts. They show what would have to be true of AI, and of India, for each future to happen.
